\begin{abstract}
We quantify how a prescribed volumetric vacuum sheath of width \(S_d\) cells loads the feed impedance of a thin-wire dipole in a three-dimensional plasma-fluid finite-difference time-domain (PF-FDTD) solver. The sheath is a conductor-attached density hole: ambient density is depleted by \(10^{-6}\) in a staircased shell of thickness \(S_d\Delta x\) about every PEC cell, so plasma conduction current cannot reach the metal and feed current must close by displacement current across the radial gap. That geometry is the FDTD analogue of a series gap between metal and exterior plasma, not a shunt feed capacitance. Continuous-wave (CW) phasors at plasma frequency \(f_p=2\,\mathrm{MHz}\) are reported for \(S_d\in\{0,2,4,6,8,10\}\) in \(1.50\)--\(2.30\,\mathrm{MHz}\), with a low-frequency extension at \(S_d=0,2,10\) (\(0.50\)--\(1.50\,\mathrm{MHz}\)). In \(1.50\)--\(1.80\,\mathrm{MHz}\), \(\mathrm{Im}\{Z\}\) at fixed frequency becomes steadily more negative as \(S_d\) increases for every \(S_d\ge 2\). Near \(f_p\) that ordering reverses: thicker jackets damp the plasma-resonance swing rather than deepen capacitive reactance. Measured \(\mathrm{Im}\{Z\}\) \(+\to-\) crossings exist for \(S_d=0\) (\(1.846\,\mathrm{MHz}\)) and \(S_d=2\) (\(0.655\,\mathrm{MHz}\), resting on a single inductive sample); \(S_d=4,6,8\) supply only upper bounds \(<1.50\,\mathrm{MHz}\), and \(S_d=10\) is capacitive throughout \(0.50\)--\(2.30\,\mathrm{MHz}\). Defining the mixed diagnostic \(C_\mathrm{eff}=-1/(\omega\,\mathrm{Im}\{Z\})\) on capacitive branches and averaging \(0.8\)--\(1.2\,\mathrm{MHz}\) gives \(C_\mathrm{eff}(2)\approx 7.4\,\mathrm{pF}\) and \(C_\mathrm{eff}(10)\approx 4.3\,\mathrm{pF}\). A coaxial estimate for a \emph{center-fed dipole} (two arms of length \(L/2\) in series) yields \(C_\mathrm{sh}^\mathrm{(dip)}\approx 6.1\) and \(3.7\,\mathrm{pF}\) for those widths, so \(C_\mathrm{eff}/C_\mathrm{sh}^\mathrm{(dip)}\approx 1.2\). Applying the single-arm monopole coax formula to a center-fed dipole overpredicts by a factor of four; equation~(\ref{eq:coax_dip}) is the correct electrostatic object for this antenna. A pedagogical circuit \(Z(S_d=0)+1/(j\omega C_\mathrm{sh}^\mathrm{(dip)})\) fails to reproduce the measured \(\mathrm{Re}\{Z\}(S_d)\) drop: the jacket both inserts a gap and removes near-wire plasma dielectric. The work is a static prescribed-jacket result on a small domain, not a claim of Tu/Song dynamic sheath fidelity.
\begin{plainlanguagesummary}
Radio antennas in space plasmas sit inside a thin empty sheath next to the metal. We simulated that empty layer as a jacket of controlled thickness around a dipole in a 3-D plasma wave code. Because the jacket wraps the wire, current has to jump the empty gap to reach the plasma; that is a series capacitor, not a capacitor across the antenna terminals. In a band below the plasma frequency, thicker jackets made the antenna look more capacitive for every thickness we ran. Near the plasma frequency the picture changes because the jacket also removes plasma near the wire. An analytic coax formula written for a dipole (not a monopole) matches the simulated capacitance to about twenty percent in a low-frequency window, but the full impedance is not a fixed plasma load plus a pure capacitor.
\end{plainlanguagesummary}
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

A conducting antenna immersed in plasma is surrounded by a depleted sheath that can dominate low-frequency impedance. For VLF dipoles in the ionosphere, Mlodnosky and Garriott showed that the feed admittance is often controlled by the plasma sheath rather than by bulk dielectric loading \cite{mlodnosky1962}. Classical short-dipole theory in a cold magnetoplasma \cite{balmain1964,balmain1969} provides the homogeneous-medium baseline. Balmain-era and related treatments then add ion sheaths, insulating jackets, and dielectric coatings as explicit \emph{series} gap loading between metal and exterior plasma \cite{galejs1966,adachi1977}. The same topology---a coaxial vacuum annulus in series with the plasma-loaded antenna---persists in modern impedance-probe and laboratory analyses that write an analytic sheath capacitor ahead of a lumped plasma term \cite{brooks2024}.

High-voltage transmitting antennas raise a related but distinct problem: Song and coworkers modeled an oscillating ion-sheath radius about a bare dipole \cite{song2007}, and Tu et al.\ used one-dimensional particle-in-cell (PIC) simulations to refine sheath structure and reactance \cite{tu2008}. Those studies motivate the statement that sheath changes antenna impedance. They address self-consistent, time-varying sheaths on high-voltage transmitters; they do not prescribe a fixed vacuum jacket in three-dimensional fluid FDTD.

This paper asks a narrower question suited to the PF-FDTD solver used here \cite{ward2006}: does a \emph{prescribed} volumetric vacuum jacket of width \(S_d\Delta x\) load a plasma-immersed dipole as an emergent \emph{series} gap, and how does that loading compare with a dipole coaxial estimate? Success is defined by (i)~monotonic capacitive ordering of \(Z(f)\) with \(S_d\) in a stated band below \(f_p\), (ii)~honest reporting of resonance markers including upper bounds, and (iii)~comparison to the correct dipole coax object after acknowledging that \(C_\mathrm{eff}\) mixes sheath and plasma reactance. An upward Im-zero shift with \(S_d\) is not a success metric for this static-jacket experiment.

\section{Methods}

\subsection{PF-FDTD solver and antenna geometry}

PF-FDTD advances Maxwell's equations on a staggered Yee grid coupled to multi-species fluid continuity and momentum equations \cite{ward2006}. Plasma current \(\mathbf{J}=\sum_s q_s n_s\mathbf{u}_s\) enters Ampere's law. The present builds use \(NS=3\) species (electrons plus two positive-ion fluids with oxygen and nitric-oxide masses), cold fluid temperature \(T=0\), and a collision ratio \(\nu/f_p=0.1\) (\(\nu=200\,\mathrm{kHz}\) at \(f_p=2\,\mathrm{MHz}\)). Unless noted, the cyclotron frequency is set to zero (effectively unmagnetized). Bulk electron density follows from \(f_p\): \(N_{0,e}\approx 4.96\times 10^{10}\,\mathrm{m}^{-3}\).

Feed observables are voltage \(V(t)\) and current \(I(t)\) at a hard \(E_z\) source. Voltage is \(V=-E_z\Delta z\); current is the Ampere-loop circulation of \(\mathbf{B}\) around the feed cell \cite{ward2006}. Input impedance
\begin{equation}
Z(f)=\frac{V(f)}{I(f)}
\end{equation}
is formed from steady-state CW phasors on the last 50\% of each \texttt{.vc} record. Source type~3 (hard field) is imposed at the feed; this is not a soft gap feed and should be treated as part of the numerical antenna model.

All runs use a \(70\times 70\times 65\) grid with \(\Delta x=\Delta y=\Delta z=0.04\,\mathrm{m}\) (domain \(\approx 2.8\times 2.8\times 2.6\,\mathrm{m}\)) and time step \(\Delta t=6.671114\times 10^{-11}\,\mathrm{s}\). Domain faces use first-order retarded absorbing boundary conditions \cite{ward2006}. A \(z\)-directed thin-wire dipole occupies grid indices \((35,35,20)\) to \((35,35,45)\) (length \(L=1.00\,\mathrm{m}\)) as a one-cell PEC line (\(\mathrm{ER}_z=0\)); the feed is at \((35,35,33)\). No Holland--Simpson thin-wire radius correction is applied inside the solver; \(r_\mathrm{eff}\) appears only in the analytic coax comparison below. Each CW tone is integrated for at least \(10^5\) steps and at least 15 drive cycles (\texttt{VcRate}=10 samples). The domain is electrically small relative to \(c/\omega_p\approx 24\,\mathrm{m}\) and to free-space wavelengths at \(0.5\)--\(2\,\mathrm{MHz}\); absolute \(Z\) values may therefore carry box and boundary contributions, which is why trend-with-\(S_d\) is the primary claim.

\subsection{Prescribed volumetric sheath as a series gap}

The sheath is imposed as a \emph{jacket that coats the conductor}, not as a circuit element connected across the feed terminals (Figure~\ref{fig:geom}). Ambient density is initialized uniformly. After the antenna permittivity flags exist, a discrete distance field is grown from every PEC cell (\(\mathrm{ER}_x\mathrm{ER}_y\mathrm{ER}_z=0\)). Cells whose distance \(d\) (in grid steps) satisfies
\begin{equation}
0 < d \le S_d
\quad\Rightarrow\quad
N_0 \rightarrow N_0\cdot 10^{-6}
\end{equation}
are depleted to a numerical floor (\(N_\mathrm{min}/N_0=10^{-6}\)). Plasma conduction current in that shell is negligible, so the jacket behaves as vacuum. All results here use this PEC-seeded implementation. Figure~\ref{fig:n0} shows a \emph{schematic} of the applied step, not a dumped field dump.

Because the depleted shell surrounds the metal, feed current reaching the bulk plasma must cross the radial gap as displacement current. A pedagogical circuit is
\begin{equation}
Z_\mathrm{in}(f)\approx Z_\mathrm{pl}(f)+\frac{1}{j\omega C_\mathrm{sh}(S_d)},
\label{eq:series}
\end{equation}
i.e.\ series sheath capacitance, not a shunt \(C\) across the terminals. Equation~(\ref{eq:series}) is a useful topology statement, not an assumption that \(Z_\mathrm{pl}\) is independent of \(S_d\): thickening the jacket also removes plasma dielectric near the wire. Thin-wire geometry favors a logarithmic coaxial form for the gap estimate.

\subsection{CW campaigns and resonance markers}

Two CW grids share the same geometry and \(f_p\):
\begin{description}
\item[Dense band (all thicknesses)] \(S_d\in\{0,2,4,6,8,10\}\), \(f=1.50\)--\(2.30\,\mathrm{MHz}\) (100~kHz and selected half steps; 66 completed cases). This is the full \(S_d\) survey and the primary loading evidence.
\item[Low-frequency extension] \(S_d\in\{0,2,10\}\), \(f=0.50\)--\(1.50\,\mathrm{MHz}\) (100~kHz steps). These three thicknesses were re-run below \(1.50\,\mathrm{MHz}\) after the dense band showed that every \(S_d\ge 2\) was already capacitive there. Intermediate widths \(S_d=4,6,8\) remain in the dense-band results; their low-frequency crossings are not yet measured and are reported only as upper bounds.
\end{description}
One dense-band sample (\(S_d=0\), \(1.50\,\mathrm{MHz}\)) is an incomplete restart file and is omitted from all plots and averages.

Series resonance \(f_\mathrm{res}\) is the first \(\mathrm{Im}\{Z\}\) positive-to-negative zero crossing by linear interpolation when both neighboring samples exist. We also report $|Z|$ in the dense band as a second marker and do not equate phase-zero with magnitude extrema. On \(\Delta x=0.04\,\mathrm{m}\), \(S_d=2\) is already an \(8\,\mathrm{cm}\) vacuum jacket---thick relative to a typical ionospheric Debye sheath at this density---so series-gap loading is intentionally strong.

\subsection{Effective capacitance and coaxial references}

Where \(\mathrm{Im}\{Z\}<0\), define the mixed diagnostic
\begin{equation}
C_\mathrm{eff}(f)=-\frac{1}{\omega\,\mathrm{Im}\{Z(f)\}},
\qquad \omega=2\pi f.
\label{eq:ceff}
\end{equation}
This is an effective feed capacitance of the full antenna--sheath--plasma system on the present grid, not a pure vacuum gap to an ideal plasma electrode. It is frequency-dependent in the data and is used only as a secondary comparison, not as the primary claim.

The standard coaxial sheath capacitance for a single cylindrical arm of length \(L\) is
\begin{equation}
C_\mathrm{sh}^\mathrm{(mono)}=\frac{2\pi\varepsilon_0 L}{\ln\!\bigl(1+S_d\Delta x/r_\mathrm{eff}\bigr)}.
\label{eq:coax_mono}
\end{equation}
For a center-fed dipole of total length \(L\), each arm of length \(L/2\) contributes half that capacitance if treated as an independent coax, and the two arms are electrically in series at the feed, so
\begin{equation}
C_\mathrm{sh}^\mathrm{(dip)}=\frac{C_\mathrm{sh}^\mathrm{(mono)}}{4}
=\frac{\pi\varepsilon_0 L}{2\ln\!\bigl(1+S_d\Delta x/r_\mathrm{eff}\bigr)}.
\label{eq:coax_dip}
\end{equation}
We take \(r_\mathrm{eff}=0.23\Delta x\) as a Holland-type thin-wire estimate for the analytic comparison only \cite{holland1981}, and also show \(r_\mathrm{eff}=0.5\Delta x\) as a staircased-radius sensitivity. The quasi-static diagnostic average uses \(0.8\)--\(1.2\,\mathrm{MHz}\) (capacitive points only) for \(S_d=2,10\). Dense-band \(C_\mathrm{eff}\) near \(f_p\) is not treated as a quasi-static capacitor.

\section{Results}

\subsection{Dense-band impedance at all \(S_d\)}

Figure~\ref{fig:zdense} shows the full thickness survey. In \(1.50\)--\(1.80\,\mathrm{MHz}\), curves are ordered with \(S_d\): larger jackets reduce \(\mathrm{Re}\{Z\}\) and drive \(\mathrm{Im}\{Z\}\) more negative at every frequency in that window. For \(S_d\ge 2\), \(\mathrm{Im}\{Z\}<0\) at all dense-band tones; only \(S_d=0\) crosses from inductive to capacitive, at \(1.846\,\mathrm{MHz}\). Near \(f_p\) (\(2.0\)--\(2.3\,\mathrm{MHz}\)) the ordering reverses: the unsheathed dipole develops the largest capacitive swing, and thicker jackets damp that plasma feature (Figure~\ref{fig:imzsd}). Intermediate widths \(S_d=4,6,8\) lie between \(S_d=2\) and \(S_d=10\) in the low dense band; they are not outliers. The same low-band ordering appears in $|Z|$ (Figure~\ref{fig:zabs}).

\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:disc}

\subsection{Series versus parallel topology}

The depleted shell sits between the conductor and bulk plasma (Figure~\ref{fig:geom}). Series topology follows from that geometry. A shunt capacitor across the feed would not thicken when \(S_d\) increases around the wire, and it would not produce the ordered capacitive family in Figure~\ref{fig:imzsd}a.

\subsection{Why low-band capacitive loading strengthens with \(S_d\)}

Thickening the jacket simultaneously (i) removes plasma dielectric near the wire and (ii) inserts series gap capacitance. In \(1.50\)--\(1.80\,\mathrm{MHz}\), the net effect is stronger capacitive \(\mathrm{Im}\{Z\}\). Near \(f_p\), removing plasma dielectric and damping the resonance dominate, so ordering reverses (Figure~\ref{fig:imzsd}b). On this grid \(S_d=2\) is already an \(8\,\mathrm{cm}\) vacuum jacket---a strong load relative to a physical Debye sheath at ionospheric temperatures---which amplifies gap effects in the low band.

\subsection{Analytic coax: monopole versus dipole}

Equation~(\ref{eq:coax_mono}) is the cylindrical coax limit used since Mlodnosky and Garriott and in later Balmain-era jacket and insulated-antenna models \cite{mlodnosky1962,galejs1966}. It describes a \emph{single} metal arm relative to exterior plasma. Applying that formula unchanged to a center-fed dipole of total length \(L\) overpredicts feed capacitance by a factor of four. With equation~(\ref{eq:coax_dip}), the diagnostic ratios become order unity. Remaining \(\sim 20\%\) offsets are consistent with staircasing, \(r_\mathrm{eff}\) ambiguity, residual plasma reactance inside \(C_\mathrm{eff}\), and the electrically small domain.

\subsection{Relation to Song et al.\ and Tu et al.}

\citeA{song2007} and \citeA{tu2008} address high-voltage transmitting dipoles with self-consistent, time-varying sheaths. The present runs prescribe a static step jacket in fluid FDTD. We therefore do not claim Tu dynamic fidelity. The appropriate static bridge is emergent gap loading and \(C_\mathrm{sh}(S_d)\), not an Im-zero scoreboard against those papers.

\subsection{Limitations}

\begin{enumerate}
\item Free-space \(f_\mathrm{res}\) on the same dipole remains an open upper anchor (run plan documented with the analysis products).
\item Intermediate widths \(S_d=4,6,8\) lack low-frequency CW; \(f_\mathrm{res}\) for those cases is only an upper bound. Denser tones around \(0.55\)--\(0.70\,\mathrm{MHz}\) for \(S_d=2\) are also open.
\item \(C_\mathrm{eff}\) mixes sheath and plasma reactance and is frequency-dependent; it is a diagnostic, not a de-embedded gap capacitance.
\item Absolute \(Z\) and \(C_\mathrm{eff}\) may include box/ABC contributions on this electrically small domain.
\item Absolute coax comparison still depends on \(r_\mathrm{eff}\); we report Holland-like \(0.23\Delta x\) and staircased \(0.5\Delta x\).
\item Campaigns here are effectively unmagnetized; PIP upper-hybrid markers require \(B\neq 0\).
\item Cold fluid \(T=0\) has no thermal Debye length; physical-sheath comparisons use ionospheric \(T_e\) only as context.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Conclusions}

A prescribed volumetric vacuum sheath in PF-FDTD loads a plasma-immersed dipole as a \textbf{series} gap because the jacket coats the wire and feed current must cross that gap by displacement current. In \(1.50\)--\(1.80\,\mathrm{MHz}\), CW measurements at all six thicknesses show monotonic capacitive loading with \(S_d\). Near \(f_p\) that ordering reverses because the jacket also modifies the plasma path. Measured Im-zero crossings decrease from \(S_d=0\) to \(S_d=2\); intermediate widths are constrained only by upper bounds. A dipole coaxial estimate matches the low-frequency \(C_\mathrm{eff}\) diagnostic to within \(\sim 20\%\), whereas a monopole coax formula is the wrong object. A fixed-\(Z_\mathrm{pl}\) series-\(C\) overlay fails for \(\mathrm{Re}\{Z\}\). These results establish emergent series-gap loading as the correct FDTD interpretation of a static jacket and provide a quantitative baseline---with stated limitations---before prescribing time-varying sheath boundaries or self-consistent charging.
