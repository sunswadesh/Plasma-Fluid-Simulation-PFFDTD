\begin{abstract}
We quantify how a prescribed volumetric vacuum sheath of width \(S_d\) cells loads the feed impedance of a thin-wire dipole in a three-dimensional plasma-fluid finite-difference time-domain (PF-FDTD) solver. The sheath is not a lumped element placed across the feed. It is a conductor-attached density hole: ambient density is depleted by \(10^{-6}\) in a staircased shell of thickness \(S_d\Delta x\) about every PEC cell, so plasma conduction current cannot reach the metal and feed current must close by displacement current across the radial gap. That geometry is the FDTD analogue of a series sheath capacitor in series with the plasma-loaded antenna, not a shunt feed capacitance. Continuous-wave (CW) phasors at plasma frequency \(f_p=2\,\mathrm{MHz}\) are reported for the full thickness set \(S_d\in\{0,2,4,6,8,10\}\) in \(1.50\)--\(2.30\,\mathrm{MHz}\). For every \(S_d\ge 2\), \(\mathrm{Im}\{Z\}<0\) throughout that band and \(\mathrm{Im}\{Z\}\) at fixed frequency becomes steadily more negative as \(S_d\) increases. A low-frequency extension at \(S_d=0,2,10\) (\(0.50\)--\(1.50\,\mathrm{MHz}\)) was run because those three thicknesses already showed that any \(\mathrm{Im}\{Z\}\) \(+\to-\) crossing for \(S_d\ge 2\) lies below \(1.50\,\mathrm{MHz}\); \(S_d=4,6,8\) were not omitted from the physics, only from the extra low-frequency grid. The first \(+\to-\) marker is \(f_\mathrm{res}(0)=1.846\,\mathrm{MHz}\), \(f_\mathrm{res}(2)=0.655\,\mathrm{MHz}\), \(f_\mathrm{res}(4,6,8)<1.50\,\mathrm{MHz}\), and \(f_\mathrm{res}(10)\le 0.50\,\mathrm{MHz}\). Defining \(C_\mathrm{eff}=-1/(\omega\,\mathrm{Im}\{Z\})\) on capacitive branches and averaging \(0.8\)--\(1.2\,\mathrm{MHz}\) gives \(C_\mathrm{eff}(2)\approx 7.4\,\mathrm{pF}\) and \(C_\mathrm{eff}(10)\approx 4.3\,\mathrm{pF}\). Dense-band averages at \(1.50\)--\(1.70\,\mathrm{MHz}\) for \(S_d=4,6,8,10\) continue the same decrease. Both sets track coaxial \(C_\mathrm{sh}=2\pi\varepsilon_0 L/\ln(1+S_d\Delta x/r_\mathrm{eff})\) in trend, with \(C_\mathrm{eff}/C_\mathrm{sh}\approx 0.3\) in the quasi-static window. The work is a static prescribed-jacket result, not a claim of Tu/Song dynamic sheath fidelity.
\begin{plainlanguagesummary}
Radio antennas in space plasmas sit inside a thin empty sheath next to the metal. We simulated that empty layer as a jacket of controlled thickness around a dipole in a 3-D plasma wave code. The jacket wraps the wire, so current has to jump the empty gap to reach the plasma; that is a series capacitor, not a capacitor across the antenna terminals. Making the jacket thicker made every thickness we simulated look more capacitive and moved the resonance to lower frequency. The effective capacitance extracted from the simulations decreases with jacket thickness in the same way as a simple coaxial capacitor formula, up to a nearly constant scale factor.
\end{plainlanguagesummary}
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

A conducting antenna immersed in plasma is surrounded by a depleted sheath that can dominate low-frequency impedance. Classical short-dipole theory in a cold magnetoplasma \cite{balmain1964,balmain1969} provides the homogeneous-medium baseline; later Balmain-era and contemporary treatments add ion sheaths, insulating jackets, and sheath-wave physics as series gap loading between metal and exterior plasma. Modern plasma impedance probe (PIP) retrievals often write an explicit coaxial sheath capacitor in series with a Balmain-like plasma term \cite{liu_pip,brooks2024}, so that the phase-zero frequency lies below bulk plasma features.

High-voltage transmitting antennas raise a related but distinct problem: Song and coworkers modeled an oscillating ion-sheath radius about a bare dipole \cite{song2007}, and Tu et al.\ used one-dimensional particle-in-cell (PIC) simulations to refine sheath structure and reactance \cite{tu2008}. Those studies motivate the statement that sheath changes antenna impedance, but they do not prescribe a fixed vacuum jacket in three-dimensional fluid FDTD, nor do they require that an \(\mathrm{Im}\{Z\}=0\) marker climb with sheath thickness.

This paper addresses a narrower, quantitative question suited to the PF-FDTD solver used here \cite{ward2006}: does a \emph{prescribed} volumetric vacuum jacket of width \(S_d\Delta x\) behave as a \emph{series} sheath capacitor whose effective \(C_\mathrm{eff}(S_d)\) tracks coaxial analytic expectations? Campaign diagnostics that established conductor-attached sheath coupling are summarized only as needed; the methodological path, including a pre-fix seeding bug, is documented elsewhere \cite{paper0}. Success is defined by \(C_\mathrm{eff}\) versus coax and multi-marker reporting of \(Z(f)\), not by an upward Im-zero shift with \(S_d\).

\section{Methods}

\subsection{PF-FDTD solver and antenna geometry}

PF-FDTD advances Maxwell's equations on a staggered Yee grid coupled to multi-species fluid continuity and momentum equations \cite{ward2006}. Plasma current enters Ampere's law; feed observables are voltage \(V(t)\) and current \(I(t)\) at a hard \(E_z\) source, with
\begin{equation}
Z(f)=\frac{V(f)}{I(f)}
\end{equation}
from steady-state CW phasors on the last 50\% of each \texttt{.vc} record.

All runs use a \(70\times 70\times 65\) grid with \(\Delta x=0.04\,\mathrm{m}\) and a \(z\)-directed thin-wire dipole from grid indices \((35,35,20)\) to \((35,35,45)\) (length \(L=1.00\,\mathrm{m}\)) with the feed at \((35,35,33)\). Unless noted, \(f_p=2\,\mathrm{MHz}\), collisions are weak, and the background magnetic field is zero.

\subsection{Prescribed volumetric sheath as a series gap}

The sheath is imposed as a \emph{jacket that coats the conductor}, not as a circuit element connected across the feed terminals (Figure~\ref{fig:geom}). Ambient density is initialized uniformly. After the antenna permittivity flags exist, a discrete distance field is grown from every PEC cell (\(\mathrm{ER}_x\mathrm{ER}_y\mathrm{ER}_z=0\)). Cells whose distance \(d\) (in grid steps) satisfies
\begin{equation}
0 < d \le S_d
\quad\Rightarrow\quad
N_0 \rightarrow N_0\cdot 10^{-6}
\end{equation}
are depleted to a numerical floor (\(N_\mathrm{min}/N_0=10^{-6}\)). Plasma conduction current in that shell is negligible, so the jacket behaves as vacuum. Seeding from the plasma-on mask rather than from PEC previously placed the hole at the domain boundary and made \(Z\) independent of \(S_d\); all results here use the PEC-seeded implementation \cite{paper0}. Figure~\ref{fig:n0} shows the resulting radial step in \(N_0\).

Because the depleted shell surrounds the metal, feed current reaching the bulk plasma must cross the radial gap as displacement current. The corresponding circuit is
\begin{equation}
Z_\mathrm{in}(f)\approx Z_\mathrm{pl}(f)+\frac{1}{j\omega C_\mathrm{sh}(S_d)},
\label{eq:series}
\end{equation}
i.e.\ series sheath capacitance, not a shunt \(C\) across the terminals. Parallel-plate estimates \(C\sim\varepsilon_0 A/(S_d\Delta x)\) are only pedagogical magnitude guides for that series gap. Thin-wire geometry favors the logarithmic coaxial form used below.

(b)~Equivalent topology: displacement current across the gap inserts \(1/(j\omega C_\mathrm{sh})\) in series with the plasma-loaded antenna \(Z_\mathrm{pl}\). \subsection{CW campaigns and why the low-frequency grid is smaller}

Two CW grids share the same geometry and \(f_p\):
\begin{description}
\item[Dense band (all thicknesses)] \(S_d\in\{0,2,4,6,8,10\}\), \(f=1.50\)--\(2.30\,\mathrm{MHz}\) (100~kHz and selected half steps; 66 completed cases). This is the full \(S_d\) survey.
\item[Low-frequency extension] \(S_d\in\{0,2,10\}\), \(f=0.50\)--\(1.50\,\mathrm{MHz}\) (100~kHz steps). These three thicknesses were re-run below \(1.50\,\mathrm{MHz}\) after the dense band showed that every \(S_d\ge 2\) was already capacitive there, so any \(+\to-\) crossing had to lie at lower frequency. Intermediate widths \(S_d=4,6,8\) remain in the dense-band results; they were not discarded.
\end{description}
One dense-band sample (\(S_d=0\), \(1.50\,\mathrm{MHz}\)) is an incomplete restart file and is omitted from all plots and averages.

Series resonance \(f_\mathrm{res}\) is the first \(\mathrm{Im}\{Z\}\) positive-to-negative zero crossing by linear interpolation. We also report $|Z|$ in the dense band as a second marker and do not equate phase-zero with magnitude extrema.

\subsection{Effective capacitance and coaxial reference}

Where \(\mathrm{Im}\{Z\}<0\), define
\begin{equation}
C_\mathrm{eff}(f)=-\frac{1}{\omega\,\mathrm{Im}\{Z(f)\}},
\qquad \omega=2\pi f.
\label{eq:ceff}
\end{equation}
This \(C_\mathrm{eff}\) is an \emph{effective} feed capacitance of the full antenna--sheath--plasma system, not a pure vacuum gap to an ideal plasma electrode. The quasi-static average uses \(0.8\)--\(1.2\,\mathrm{MHz}\) (capacitive points only), away from the narrow inductive island of \(S_d=2\) near \(0.60\,\mathrm{MHz}\). That window exists only for the low-frequency set \(S_d=2,10\). For \(S_d=4,6,8\) (and as a consistency check on \(S_d=10\)) we also average \(C_\mathrm{eff}\) over \(1.50\)--\(1.70\,\mathrm{MHz}\), the lowest dense-band frequencies still well below \(f_p\). We do not use the dense-band \(C_\mathrm{eff}\) for \(S_d=2\), where \(\mathrm{Im}\{Z\}\) is approaching zero and equation~(\ref{eq:ceff}) is no longer a quasi-static capacitor.

The coaxial analytic sheath for a cylinder of length \(L\) and effective radius \(r_\mathrm{eff}\) is
\begin{equation}
C_\mathrm{sh}^\mathrm{(coax)}=\frac{2\pi\varepsilon_0 L}{\ln\!\bigl(1+S_d\Delta x/r_\mathrm{eff}\bigr)}.
\label{eq:coax}
\end{equation}
We take \(r_\mathrm{eff}=0.23\Delta x\) as a Holland-type thin-wire estimate on the Yee lattice \cite{holland1981}; sensitivity to \(r_\mathrm{eff}\) is discussed in Section~\ref{sec:disc}.

\section{Results}

\subsection{Dense-band impedance at all \(S_d\)}

Figure~\ref{fig:zdense} shows the full thickness survey. Curves are ordered with \(S_d\): larger jackets reduce \(\mathrm{Re}\{Z\}\) and drive \(\mathrm{Im}\{Z\}\) more negative at every frequency in \(1.50\)--\(1.80\,\mathrm{MHz}\). For \(S_d\ge 2\), \(\mathrm{Im}\{Z\}<0\) at all dense-band tones; only \(S_d=0\) crosses from inductive to capacitive, at \(1.846\,\mathrm{MHz}\). The same ordering appears in $|Z|$ (Figure~\ref{fig:zabs}): the dense-band magnitude peak collapses monotonically as the jacket thickens. Intermediate widths \(S_d=4,6,8\) lie between \(S_d=2\) and \(S_d=10\); they are not outliers and are not omitted from the loading argument.

Low-frequency ratios \(C_\mathrm{eff}/C_\mathrm{sh}\) are \(0.30\) (\(S_d=2\)) and \(0.29\) (\(S_d=10\)). Dense-band ratios for \(S_d=4,6,8,10\) lie in \(0.29\)--\(0.37\). Absolute scale therefore depends on \(r_\mathrm{eff}\), staircasing, and residual plasma reactance inside \(C_\mathrm{eff}\), but the \emph{trend} with \(S_d\) matches series coaxial loading: thicker jackets reduce capacitance and strengthen capacitive reactance at fixed frequency.

\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:disc}

\subsection{Series versus parallel topology}

The depleted shell sits between the conductor and bulk plasma (Figure~\ref{fig:geom}). Equation~(\ref{eq:series}) follows from that geometry, not from a post-hoc circuit fit. A shunt capacitor across the feed would not thicken when \(S_d\) increases around the wire, and it would not produce the ordered capacitive family in Figure~\ref{fig:zdense}.

\subsection{Why \(f_\mathrm{res}\) falls with \(S_d\)}

Thickening the jacket simultaneously (i) removes plasma dielectric near the wire (favoring an upward shift toward free space) and (ii) inserts series \(C_\mathrm{sh}\) (favoring a downward phase-zero shift, as in PIP \(f_{\phi0}\) arguments). Under the \(\mathrm{Im}\{Z\}\) crossing metric on this grid, series capacitance wins. On \(\Delta x=0.04\,\mathrm{m}\), \(S_d=2\) is already an \(8\,\mathrm{cm}\) vacuum jacket---a strong capacitive load relative to a physical Debye sheath---which amplifies series-\(C\) domination.

\subsection{Relation to Tu et al.\ and Song et al.}

\citeA{tu2008} and \citeA{song2007} address high-voltage transmitting dipoles with self-consistent, time-varying sheaths. The present runs prescribe a static step jacket in fluid FDTD. We therefore do not claim Tu dynamic fidelity. The appropriate static bridge is \(C_\mathrm{sh}(r_s)\) (and later, in follow-on work, prescribed \(\dot{r}_s\)), not an upward Im-zero scoreboard.

\subsection{Limitations}

\begin{enumerate}
\item Free-space \(f_\mathrm{res}\) on the same dipole remains an open upper anchor for the unloading narrative.
\item \(C_\mathrm{eff}\) mixes sheath and plasma reactance; a pure gap extraction would require additional modeling (e.g., vacuum reference or circuit de-embedding).
\item Absolute coax comparison depends on \(r_\mathrm{eff}\); the reported ratio \(\approx 0.3\) is for \(r_\mathrm{eff}=0.23\Delta x\). Larger \(r_\mathrm{eff}\) raises \(C_\mathrm{sh}\) further; smaller \(r_\mathrm{eff}\) improves absolute agreement but does not change the shared \(S_d\) trend.
\item Campaigns here are effectively unmagnetized; PIP upper-hybrid markers require \(B\neq 0\).
\item Low-frequency \(C_\mathrm{eff}\) exists only for \(S_d=2\) and \(10\). Intermediate widths enter through the dense band, where \(C_\mathrm{eff}\) is a more mixed observable. That is a sampling choice, not evidence against those jackets.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Conclusions}

A prescribed volumetric vacuum sheath in PF-FDTD loads a plasma-immersed dipole as a \textbf{series} capacitor because the jacket coats the wire and feed current must cross the gap by displacement current. CW measurements at all six thicknesses show monotonic capacitive loading with \(S_d\). \(\mathrm{Im}\{Z\}\) zero-crossings decrease with \(S_d\) where they can be located, and band-averaged \(C_\mathrm{eff}(S_d)\) decreases in parallel with coaxial analytic \(C_\mathrm{sh}(S_d)\) at a scale factor \(\approx 0.3\) in the quasi-static window. These results validate series-gap loading as the correct FDTD interpretation of a static jacket and provide the quantitative baseline needed before prescribing time-varying sheath boundaries or self-consistent charging.
