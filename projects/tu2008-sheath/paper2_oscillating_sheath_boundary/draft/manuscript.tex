\title{Prescribed Dynamic Sheath Versus Static Vacuum Jacket in Three-Dimensional Plasma Fluid FDTD: Measurable Dynamic Feed Loading}

\authors{A. Author\affil{1}, B. Author\affil{1}}

\affiliation{1}{Institution, City, Country}

\correspondingauthor{A. Author}{author@example.edu}

\begin{keypoints}
\item A time-varying depleted radius is imposed in three-dimensional plasma fluid FDTD without particle-in-cell.
\item Oscillating sheath cases sit far outside static thickness brackets at \(0.5\)--\(1.2\,\mathrm{MHz}\) (soft updates).
\item Feed \(\arg(Z)\) tracks kinematic boundary phase over \(\sim 70^\circ\); amplitude scaling with sheath swing remains unsettled.
\end{keypoints}

\begin{abstract}
Radio-frequency antennas in plasma are surrounded by a depleted near-zone layer whose thickness need not be frozen over a drive cycle.
Most three-dimensional fluid electromagnetic models either omit that layer or treat it as a static vacuum jacket.
Here we keep the Maxwell--fluid core fixed and prescribe a kinematic sheath radius \(r_s(t)=r_{s0}+\Delta r\sin(\omega t+\phi)\), comparing feed diagnostics to static jackets at the mean and extreme thicknesses.
At \(700\,\mathrm{kHz}\) (\(f_p=2\,\mathrm{MHz}\)), hard-staircase and soft-edge updates both place the oscillating phasor impedance far outside the static bracket segment in the complex plane.
Varying the soft blend width over \(0.5\)--\(2\) cells moves the oscillating point but keeps it out-of-bracket.
A soft sparse frequency set at \(0.5\), \(0.7\), and \(1.2\,\mathrm{MHz}\) likewise remains out-of-bracket at all three tones.
A phase sweep of the prescribed motion rotates feed \(\arg(Z)\) by tens of degrees while staying out-of-bracket.
A soft-edge \(\Delta r\) scan, and a matched re-scan with \(\mathrm{soft}=\Delta r\), keep large out-of-bracket offsets at several amplitudes but do not yield a clean monotonic amplitude law.
The results show that a changing prescribed density hole loads the feed differently from static thickness sampling in this engine.
Song/Tu-type works motivate that real RF sheaths move; we do not treat their analytics or PIC reactance as a numerical scoreboard.
\end{abstract}

\section*{Plain Language Summary}
Antennas in space plasmas sit inside a low-density sheath.
In strong radio-frequency drive that sheath can breathe---growing and shrinking each cycle---instead of staying a fixed gap.
We force that breathing as a known function of time inside a three-dimensional fluid electromagnetic code and compare the antenna voltage and current to cases with a fixed gap.
The breathing cases look electrically different from the fixed gaps, even after we smooth the numerical sheath edge.
That means sheath motion itself can matter for how the antenna loads, before any self-consistent charging model is added.

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

A depleted sheath between a metallic antenna and the ambient plasma inserts a largely reactive series impedance and can dominate feed behavior at ionospheric and magnetospheric radio frequencies
\cite{balmain1964,liu_sheath,song2007,tu2008}.
Probe-oriented models often treat that layer as a quasi-steady coaxial (or related) capacitance in series with the plasma impedance
\cite{liu_sheath}.
High-voltage transmitting antennas, by contrast, are expected to develop a sheath whose outer radius moves with the drive
\cite{song2007,tu2008}.

Three-dimensional plasma fluid FDTD (PF-FDTD) can already impose a \emph{static} volumetric density hole of width \(S_d\) around a thin-wire dipole and measure feed impedance
\cite{ward2006}.
Companion campaign work showed strong series-capacitance loading once the hole was seeded from the conductor surface (static sheath capacitance study in preparation).
Varying static \(S_d\) samples different quasi-steady thicknesses; it does not test whether \emph{time dependence} of the hole changes the feed beyond those thicknesses.

This paper asks a physics question about consequences of motion, not a reproduction of Song or Tu numerics:
if \(r_s(t)\) is \emph{prescribed} to oscillate at the drive frequency inside the same Maxwell--fluid core, do feed diagnostics differ from static jackets at \(r_{s0}\) and \(r_{s0}+\Delta r\)?
A positive answer means dynamic depletion is electromagnetically consequential in this solver class.
We do not claim self-consistent charging, orbit-limited collection, or an electron-free ion sheath---only a kinematic density hole.

\section{Model and Methods}
\label{sec:methods}

\subsection{Plasma fluid FDTD}
\label{sec:pffdtd}

We advance Maxwell's equations on a staggered Yee grid coupled to multi-species fluid continuity and momentum equations following Ward~\cite{ward2006}.
Plasma--Maxwell coupling uses a conductivity mask \(\mathrm{SIG}\); conductors use permittivity flags \(\mathrm{ER}_*=0\).
Feed voltage and current are recorded at the dipole gap.
Unless noted, \(f_p=2\,\mathrm{MHz}\) and \(\Delta x=0.04\,\mathrm{m}\) match the companion static-sheath campaign.

\subsection{Kinematic sheath radius}
\label{sec:rst}

Ambient density \(N_0(\mathbf{r})\) is depleted inside a neighborhood of the PEC antenna measured by a PEC-seeded integer distance field.
For oscillating runs,
\begin{equation}
r_s(t)=r_{s0}+\Delta r\sin(2\pi f_{\mathrm{osc}}t+\phi),
\label{eq:rst}
\end{equation}
with \(r_{s0}\) and \(\Delta r\) in grid cells and \(f_{\mathrm{osc}}\) defaulting to the RF drive.

Two update policies are compared.
\emph{Hard staircase:} \(S_d(t)=\mathrm{round}[r_s(t)]\); apply a step density floor when the integer width changes (and floor dynamic density in newly covered cells).
\emph{Soft edge (default for later runs):} blend \(N_0\) over a transition of width \(\mathrm{SheathSoftEdge}\) cells centered on continuous \(r_s(t)\), without hard-flooring dynamic \(N\) on each update.
When \(\Delta r=0\), only the static sheath path runs, preserving the Paper~1-style jacket.

\subsection{Experimental design}
\label{sec:design}

At each drive frequency we compare static \(r_{s0}\), static \(r_{s0}+\lceil\Delta r\rceil\), and oscillating equation~\eqref{eq:rst}.
Primary observable: fundamental phasor \(Z=V/I\) on the last half of each CW record.
Success is a clear out-of-bracket difference (dynamic vs static thickness), not a fit to Song/Tu reactance.

Baseline pilots use \(f=700\,\mathrm{kHz}\), \(r_{s0}=4\), \(\Delta r=1\), \(\phi=0\), and \(4.3\times 10^{5}\) steps.
A soft-edge \(\Delta r\) scan extends amplitudes \(\{0.5,1,1.5,2\}\) with static brackets through \(S_d=6\).

\label{sec:deltar}

Soft-edge oscillating runs at \(\Delta r\in\{0.5,1,1.5,2\}\) remain far from their static brackets, but the relative offset and \(|Z|\) are \emph{not} monotonic in \(\Delta r\) (Figure~\ref{fig:deltar}; companion analysis note).

\subsection{Soft-edge width sensitivity}
\label{sec:softedge}

\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

Hard and soft pilots reject the null that kinematic \(r_s(t)\) is indistinguishable from static sampling of \(r_{s0}\) and \(r_{s0}+\Delta r\) at this tone.
That is the changing-versus-static physics question for a prescribed density hole in 3D PF-FDTD.
A sparse frequency set confirms the same qualitative conclusion at \(500\,\mathrm{kHz}\) and \(1.2\,\mathrm{MHz}\).
A phase scan shows \(\arg(Z)\) tracks the boundary phase over tens of degrees, consistent with coherent dynamic coupling.

Caveats are first-class, not footnotes.
(i)~Motion is imposed, not caused by charge---we test consequences of a moving hole, not sheath formation.
(ii)~Soft \(I(t)\) is cleaner than hard staircase but still not purely sinusoidal; part of any single phasor may reflect residual mask updates.
(iii)~Both fixed-soft and matched-soft \(\Delta r\) scans are non-monotonic; amplitude physics remains open as a documented finding.
(iv)~Soft-edge width moves the oscillating point without removing the out-of-bracket conclusion at \(\Delta r=1\).
(v)~The depleted region is a kinematic density floor, not a kinetic electron-free ion sheath
\cite{song2007,tu2008}.

Song~\cite{song2007} and Tu et al.~\cite{tu2008} motivate that RF sheaths move and that dynamics can matter; they are not used here as a fitting target.
Optional next controls are frozen-\(r_s\) snapshots and effective-radius static nulls---not a jump to self-consistent charging unless those controls stay coherent.

\section{Conclusions}
\label{sec:conclusions}

A prescribed oscillating density hole in three-dimensional PF-FDTD produces feed impedance clearly outside static thickness brackets at \(700\,\mathrm{kHz}\), for both hard-staircase and soft-edge updates.
Soft-edge width sensitivity, a sparse frequency set (\(0.5\)--\(1.2\,\mathrm{MHz}\)), a phase scan (\(\arg Z\) span \(\sim 72^\circ\)), and matched soft \(\Delta r\) scans keep that qualitative conclusion and favor coherent boundary-driven loading.
Amplitude scaling with sheath swing remains non-monotonic even under matched soft edges.
Changing sheath geometry is therefore electromagnetically consequential in this model class; optional stronger nulls can further separate motion from instantaneous thickness sampling.
